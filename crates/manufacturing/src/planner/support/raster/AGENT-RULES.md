# Support centerline realization rules

Own the private physical-support centerline and process-role realization consumed by the parent support planner. Inputs are immutable bed-certified clearance-clipped support union S, interface ownership I, recipe and actual scheduled layer. Never access native M, image parameters, schedule construction or object ownership here.

Erode S once by declared half-bead width. Every nominal support bead remains confined to physical S. Interface is a centerline ownership/density selector, not an independently cleared physical solid: hatch ownership is E(S) intersect I versus E(S) minus I. Interface uses full density; ordinary support retains declared density. Beads may overlap a role seam; each emitted path belongs to exactly one role and is accounted once. A sub-bead I strip must not erase the physical support wall or create artificial clearance.

Before any wall/stroke authority, optional convex ears or bounded inward/Steiner moves may resolve sub-command geometry or genuine original-I spans. Search coordinates grant no authority: every replacement segment needs complete containment in unchanged E(S), preserved simple rings/hole/component topology and exact original-I role feasibility. Inward moves also prove containment in the current proposal and an empty discarded pocket, excluding only the replaced local vertices. Feasibility and wall construction share analytic role cuts and midpoint ownership; same-role tangencies grant no permission. Preserve original S/I and hatches; never filter required object/skin or admitted strokes. Construct every resulting exterior/hole wall in order, splitting only at genuine role changes without internal seam outlines. Fit these frozen proposals within 0.001 mm of every original span, retaining endpoints, closure, width, physical interval and role boundaries. Select complete command-admissible suffixes rather than strand short remainders greedily. Fitted chords stay inside original E(S); never bridge holes or merge roles. Where no complete admissible chain exists, keep the admissible chords covering the most original length, omit only the remaining sub-minimum edges (splitting the wall; a closed ring continues through its seam) and return the omitted length for the layer's `support_omitted_wall_mm` report; never invent a chord to avoid omission. Numerical degeneracy still refuses; actual strokes still require parent clearance and preceding-deposition anchor proofs.

Return staged paths without changing the parent's segment count. The parent serially attempts preferred and orthogonal axes, dropping a failed candidate before another attempt. When neither axis anchors every stroke on actual preceding support/interface deposition, it rebuilds rather than retains the axis with the smaller worst unanchored span (preferred on a tie), prints it as authored and reports that span in `unsupported_overhang_mm`: support is build-plate only and never refuses for its own anchors. This module's proof preserves the exact capsule, endpoint, overhang and bounded bridge rules; ownership regions themselves are never anchors.

Charge physical/partition contours, staged path points, wall-crossing and hatch-hit scratch, and anchor interval records against the remaining geometry allowance. Reuse exact original-I feasibility only for unchanged directed edges; charge cache storage/lookups/shifts and invalidate both directions of replacement chords. Reserve combined object/support segments before allocation. Share the parent's cumulative construction-work budget across all Boolean/projection/raster/anchor attempts; check cancellation during each stage, row/edge and anchor query. Borrow previous strokes; do not copy the motion plan or retain alternate candidates.

Parent-run evidence: private support raster tests for changing sub-bead interface ownership, unchanged exterior/hole walls, missing actual anchors and bounded staging/cancellation; unchanged native box/hole image support success plus lower-corridor blockage through public compilation and independent replay. Software admission does not establish physical strength or printer readiness.
